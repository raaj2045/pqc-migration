Fig. 5: Per-signature amortized verification cost as a function of batch size for secp256k1 and ML-DSA-65, on a log-log scale across batches of 10, 100, and 1000 signatures.
Median of 10 Go testing.B runs per (key type, batch size), each with testing.B's own iteration count, on an AMD Ryzen 5 7600X with 6 logical CPUs under Go 1.26.5; ML-DSA-65 is measured through the Cosmos SDK v0.55 key type the chain uses, which re-parses the packed key on every call. Neither scheme has native batch verification, so a batch is verified one signature after another; total batch time is in results.json.
At a batch size of 1000, ML-DSA-65 costs 39 microseconds per signature against 97 microseconds for secp256k1, a 2.4x advantage (2.2x at a batch of 10) in line with single verifications of short messages in Fig. 3, and no batching speedup for either scheme.
